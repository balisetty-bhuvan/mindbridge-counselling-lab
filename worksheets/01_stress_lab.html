Prompts for Boys' Batches

  • Performance Framing: "Think of this meter like an athlete's heart-rate monitor or a bike's RPM gauge—checking your stress early is smart self-management, not weakness."
  • Group Normalization: "Notice how many students landed in Mild or High Tension. Even the quietest guys in the hostel carry exam or routine pressure."
  • Action Pivot: "Before we learn how to reset the gauge, let's map where pressure shows up physically in the body."

Prompts for Girls' Batches

  • Safe Collective Space: "Without naming anyone, look at how much stress this room shares. Seeing those bars means nobody here is strange or alone."
  • Emotional Validation: "Missing family, roommate dynamics, and exam expectations can build up quietly—naming your number from 0 to 10 is already step one."
  • Gentle Bridge: "Let's discover how our nervous system talks to us through body clues in Stage 2."

References & Credits

Worksheet Conceptualized & Created By

B. Bhuvan, School Counsellor, M.Sc. Psychology, DCGC, NCERT

This interactive group counseling worksheet translates established, peer-reviewed psychological frameworks into standardized classroom activities suitable for residential school adolescents (Classes 6–12). Identical scales, stimulus cards, and scoring indices ensure cross-batch comparability while maintaining 100% student anonymity (only aggregate group tallies are recorded).

  • Stage 1 — Subjective Units of Distress Scale (SUDS): Wolpe, J. (1969). The Practice of Behavior Therapy. Pergamon Press. Adapted into a 4-zone anonymous classroom pulse (0–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–10) with standardized midpoint weighting for group distress monitoring.
  • Stage 2 — Somatic Emotional Mapping & ANS Physiology: Nummenmaa, L., Glerean, E., Hari, R., & Hietanen, J. K. (2014). Bodily maps of emotions. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS), 111(2), 646–651. Paired with sympathetic nervous system psychoeducation and classroom-ready grounding resets.
  • Stage 3 — Locus of Control & Transactional Coping Theory: Rotter, J. B. (1966). Generalized expectancies for internal versus external control of reinforcement. Psychological Monographs, 80(1), 1–28; and Lazarus, R. S., & Folkman, S. (1984). Stress, Appraisal, and Coping. Springer Publishing. Operationalizes coping fit between problem-focused action and emotion-focused acceptance.
  • Stage 4 — Autonomic Emotion Regulation (Paced Breathing): Gross, J. J. (1998). The emerging field of emotion regulation: An integrative review. Review of General Psychology, 2(3), 271–299. Implements 4-4-4-4 paced breathing as an evidence-based physiological response-modulation technique.

Ethical Note: Designed for preventive psychoeducation, group facilitation, and anonymized school wellness reporting—not individual clinical diagnosis.
